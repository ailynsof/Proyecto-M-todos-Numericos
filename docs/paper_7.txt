Abstract
A new family of iterative methods for finding multiple roots of nonlinear problems is proposed. The family is derivative-free and optimal in the sense of Kung–Traub’s conjecture.
The two-step family includes a weight function. The stability analysis uses two different
weight functions: a polynomial and a rational function. Furthermore, a numerical benchmark
is carried out on these two weight functions, showing the competitiveness concerning similar
methods in the literature.
Keywords Weight function · Derivative-free method · Multiple roots · Optimal
convergence · Complex dynamics

1 Introduction
Numerous problems in different fields of science, engineering, economics, and other disciplines require the solution of a nonlinear equation or a system of nonlinear equations. Some
of these problems are the determination of orbits of artificial satellites (Arroyo et al. 2012;
Chicharro et al. 2013), the relationship between prices of goods and relative remunerations
(Tramontana and Gardini 2021), the models of continuous stirred tank chemical reactors
(Cordero et al. 2020; Zafar et al. 2018) or obtaining the wavelength that maximizes the
energy density given by Planck’s radiation law (Argyros et al. 2019; Bradie 2006), among
others.

In general, nonlinear equations and systems lack an analytical solution, so one of the
alternatives presented for their resolution is iterative techniques. In recent decades, numerous strategies for obtaining such solutions have proliferated in the scientific community, as
endorsed by the books (Amat and Busquier 2016; Argyros et al. 2018; Argyros and Magreñán
2017; Cortés et al. 2014; Petković et al. 2013; Rowe 2017; Tabatabaei and Bizon 2021).
Let f : D ⊆ R −→ R be the nonlinear function whose solution α we wish to obtain. It
is well known that if the solution is simple, f (α) = 0 and f  (α)  = 0, while if the solution
has multiplicity m, the evaluations of f and its derivatives on α are 0 until reaching the m-th
derivative, which satisfies f (m) (α)  = 0. Traditional methods, such as Newton’s method, may
struggle or fail to converge when applied to such problems, requiring the development of
robust iterative techniques specifically designed to handle the complexities associated with
multiple roots. In this sense, one of the most used methods for locating multiple roots is the
modified Newton’s method, which exhibits quadratic convergence and is given by

Recently, the authors in Arora et al. (2024), Kumar et al. (2022), Behl et al. (2021b,
2022a, b, 2023), Behl (2022), Kumar et al. (2023b), Singh and Sharma (2024) and Zafar
et al. (2023a, b) developed families of derivative-free iterative methods that incorporate weight
functions. Furthermore, these methods are optimal in the sense of the Kung–Traub conjecture
(Kung and Traub 1974), which states that the convergence order p of a memoryless method
that uses d distinct functional evaluations per iteration satisfies p ≤ 2d−1 . According to the
conjecture, methods achieving this equality are called optimal.
Inspired by these results, an optimal family of derivative-free iterative methods based
on weight functions for solving nonlinear equations with multiple roots is proposed in this
paper. This family maintains a simple structure, requires only three functional evaluations,
and allows for a wide variety of methods to be obtained by selecting appropriate weight
functions.

3 Stability of the introduced family
Section 2 presented a fourth-order optimal family of iterative schemes with a weight function
and a parameter. This section performs a dynamical analysis of the proposed iterative family
using tools from complex dynamics. Theorems 1 and 2 show the theoretical order that all
the methods belonging to family (1) have under the conditions presented. However, different
methods would be obtained for different weight functions and varying the values of parameter
β, which, while keeping their optimal order of convergence, could have very different stability
properties. For this reason, we now study how the value of parameter β affects the stability
of the associated methods, i.e., the set of initial estimates for which the family’s methods
converge to the problem’s multiple roots. Previously, we describe some tools of complex
dynamics that are necessary for the study.

3.1 Basics on complex dynamics
The stability of iterative schemes is commonly analyzed using complex dynamical tools.
In the following we describe the most relevant aspects of complex dynamics to analyze
the rational function obtained after applying the iterative method under study to solve the
nonlinear equation f (z) = 0, where f : C −→ C. More precise information concerning
this study can be found in Devaney (1989) and Blanchard (1984).
Let R : Ĉ −→ Ĉ be a rational function, where Ĉ denotes the Riemann sphere. The orbit
of a point z 0 ∈ Ĉ is defined as the set of the successive applications of the operator starting
from this point, i.e., {z 0 , R(z 0 ), . . . , R n (z 0 ), . . .}. A fixed point of R satisfies R(z) = z.
Fixed points z F ∈ Ĉ are classified according to their asymptotic behavior based on the value
of the fixed point multiplier, |R  (z F )|. Depending on whether |R  (z F )| is greater, less, or
equal to 1, the fixed point is classified as attracting, repelling, or neutral, respectively. When
|R  (z F )| = 0, the fixed point is considered to have a superattracting behavior. Furthermore, if
a fixed point z F is different from the roots of the nonlinear function f (z), ideally a low-degree
polynomial, it is called a strange fixed point.
For an attracting fixed point z ∗ , the set of its successive preimages of any order defines its
basin of attraction, denoted as
A(z ∗ ) = {z 0 ∈ Ĉ : R n (z 0 ) −→ z ∗ , n → ∞}.

The Fatou set is the set of points whose orbit tends to an attracting fixed point. Its complementary on the Riemann sphere is the Julia set, which covers all repelled points and establishes
the boundaries between the basins of attraction.
Different graphical tools allow us to visualize some of the dynamical properties of the
methods. The dynamical plane is used to observe the basins of attraction of the fixed points
of the rational operator. This plane is obtained by dividing the complex plane into a mesh of
points and taking each of these points as an initial estimate. After the successive application of
the operator, the orbit of the point is analyzed and represented with different colors depending
on the point to which they converge. In this way, all the initial estimates converging to the
same point can be visualized: the basins of attraction and the Julia and Fatou sets.
A critical point z C ∈ Ĉ of R satisfies R  (z C ) = 0. Free critical points are those critical
points different from the roots of f (z). The knowledge of the critical points is relevant since
each invariant component of Fatou’s set is associated with at least one critical point (Fatou
1920; Julia 1918).
The stability of the parametric family (1) is discussed below. For this purpose, we will
select two different weight functions that satisfy Theorem 2. These weight functions are
a polynomial function and a rational function to ensure that the final fixed point operator
R is a rational function and that complex dynamics theory can be applicable. In addition to
comparing the results obtained for each family, we can determine the values of β that provide
the best members in terms of stability.

5 Conclusions
An iterative procedure has been introduced to find the multiple roots of nonlinear problems
that may be differentiable or not. The family has been constructed using divided differences
to avoid using the derivative and the weight function technique. Every family member is
optimal in the sense of Kung–Traub’s conjecture. The stability analysis evidences the regions
where the method converges for two specific weight functions and low-degree polynomials.
The numerical tests show the competitiveness of the introduced family in relation to other
contributions in the literature.
Future research could focus on the extension of this derivative-free family of iterative
methods to the multidimensional case, replacing the scalar divided differences by its vector
version and the scalar weight function by a matrix weight function.
